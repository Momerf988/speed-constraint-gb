# Gap statement (version 2, corrected against the literature review)

Speed management is well studied. The relationship between mean traffic speed and casualties
has been estimated repeatedly, most recently by Elvik, Vadeby, Hels and van Schagen (2019),
and case-control studies link a driver's own speed to crash risk (Kloeden et al. 1997, 2001,
2002). Speed-limiting technology has also been appraised for Great Britain. Carsten and Tate
(2005) estimated mandatory ISA from mean-speed rules. Tate and Carsten (2008) applied
individual risk curves to field-trial speed profiles by speed limit, and Lai, Carsten and Tate
(2012) used the same trial data. TRL (2024) appraised overridable ISA for the Department for
Transport. Full speed-limit compliance has been estimated for Sweden (Vadeby 2023) and for two
US cities (a 2025 study in Traffic Injury Prevention). Wales (2022) and Scotland (2024) have
priced the travel time lost to lower speed limits against the casualty savings, using TAG
values.

What is missing is narrower. The GB estimates of speed-limiting technology rest on speed data
from before 2010 (1998 survey data and mid-2000s field trials), and none of these reports
links to published code. No study found compares a flat vehicle speed cap with full
posted-limit compliance on the same data and models, or prices the lawful travel time that a
cap below the posted limit would remove. No study found reports how far its scenarios lie
outside the range of speed changes the models were fitted on, although that range is narrow
(Elvik et al. 2019) and recent work finds the models predict poorly at low speeds (Ambros and
Kieć 2025). The 2026 Road Safety Strategy cites TRL's estimate for 18 vehicle technologies but
does not say what share of its 65% target speed-limiting technology could deliver. No study
found estimates what full compliance or a cap would add beyond the overridable ISA that GB
decided in 2026 to mandate.

This project addresses those points for Great Britain. It builds an open, reproducible estimate
of both policies on 2010-2025 severity-adjusted STATS19, under the same speed-risk models. It
reports the extrapolation distance of every scenario, and gives ranges rather than point
estimates outside the evidence. It prices each policy's travel-time cost with TAG values,
including the lawful time a cap removes. It also states the casualty change as a share of the
2035 target, against a baseline that includes the overridable ISA that GB has decided to
mandate.
